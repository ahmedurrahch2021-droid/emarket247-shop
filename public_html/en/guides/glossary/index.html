Jewellery glossary: gold-tone, city gold & traditional terms

Plain-language definitions for the words you meet when buying gold-tone jewellery in Bangladesh — from “city gold” and “micron” to jhumka and shitahar.

In short

“Gold-tone”, “gold-plated”, “imitation”, and “city gold” all describe the same idea: a gold-colour finish over a base metal, not solid gold. This glossary explains those material terms honestly, plus the stones, finishes, and traditional Bengali piece names you will see across the shop.

Gold, metal & plating

Gold, metal & plating

  • Solid goldGold all the way through, measured in karats (18K, 22K, 24K) and stamped with a hallmark. It holds resale value. eMarket247 does not sell solid gold.
  • Karat (K)The purity of gold — 24K is pure, 22K and 18K are alloyed for strength. Not the same as “carat”, which measures gemstone weight.
  • HallmarkAn official stamp certifying gold purity. Gold-tone and imitation jewellery is not hallmarked, because it is not solid gold.
  • Gold-toneA gold-colour finish over a base metal — it may contain a little real gold or none at all. This is the honest umbrella term we use for our jewellery.
  • Gold-platedA thin layer of real gold electroplated over a base metal such as brass. It looks like gold but wears over time.
  • Gold-filledA thicker layer of gold mechanically bonded to a base metal — more durable than plating, and regulated in some countries. Uncommon in the local market.
  • Rolled goldAn older term for a thin sheet of gold bonded to a base metal. Similar idea to gold-filled.
  • Imitation jewelleryJewellery made to look like gold or gemstone pieces using base metals and simulated stones. Also called artificial or fashion jewellery.
  • City goldA common Bangladeshi term (সিটি গোল্ড) for gold-plated imitation jewellery. It is NOT real gold — the name only refers to the gold-like look.
  • One-gram goldA marketing term for jewellery plated with roughly one gram of gold over a base metal. The body of the piece is not gold.
  • MicronThe unit for plating thickness (one-thousandth of a millimetre). Higher micron means the colour lasts longer. We do not quote micron counts we cannot verify from the supplier.
  • BrassA copper-zinc alloy — the most common base metal under gold-tone plating. It can leave a green mark when the plating wears thin.
  • AlloyA blend of metals. Most imitation jewellery bodies are zinc or brass alloys chosen for shape and cost.
  • Base metalThe non-precious metal beneath the plating (usually brass or zinc alloy). It gives the piece its shape but has no gold value.
  • PlatingThe surface coating — gold, silver, or rhodium colour — applied over a base metal by electroplating.
  • TarnishA dull film that forms on the surface from air and moisture. It often polishes off, unlike plating loss which is permanent.

Stones & finishes

Stones & finishes

  • American Diamond (AD)A colourless synthetic stone (usually cubic zirconia) used to imitate diamond. Bright and affordable — not a real diamond.
  • Cubic Zirconia (CZ)The lab-made stone behind most “American Diamond” jewellery. Durable, clear, and sparkly.
  • KundanA traditional setting where stones or glass are held by thin gold-colour foil. A signature of bridal and Rajasthani-style jewellery.
  • PolkiUncut, unfaceted stones (traditionally diamond) set Kundan-style. Imitation polki uses glass or CZ for the same look.
  • MeenakariColourful enamel work on the front or back of a piece — common in traditional South-Asian jewellery.
  • Rhodium platingA silver-white plating from the platinum family, used to give a white-gold or bright silver look.
  • Antique finishA darkened, matte gold-tone finish that mimics aged temple jewellery.
  • OxidisedA blackened silver-tone finish popular in festival, boho, and everyday jewellery.

Traditional pieces

Traditional pieces (Bangla names)

  • Jhumka (ঝুমকা)Bell- or dome-shaped drop earrings — a South-Asian classic worn for both festivals and everyday.
  • Nolok (নোলক)A traditional nose ornament worn on the septum, common in Bengali bridal looks.
  • Nath / Nathni (নথ)A nose ring, often large and decorative for weddings.
  • Tikli / Mang tika (টিকলি)A forehead ornament that hangs from the hair parting.
  • Shitahar (সীতাহার)A long, layered necklace traditionally worn by Bengali brides.
  • Chik (চিক)A close-fitting, choker-style necklace that sits high on the neck.
  • Ranihaar (রানীহার)A long, grand “queen’s necklace”, often layered for bridal and festive wear.
  • Bala (বালা)A solid, rounded bangle, usually worn as a pair.
  • Churi (চুড়ি)Bangles, often worn in stacks — the Bengali word for bangles.
  • Payel / Nupur (পায়েল / নূপুর)Anklets, sometimes strung with small bells.
  • ChokerA short necklace that sits high and snug on the neck.
